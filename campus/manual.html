01 · Own the opportunity

this isn't an ambassador program. you run the campus.

we're building fomo school by school. Campus Leads bring it into the communities that shape their school. you decide how. five roles per campus, sixty days. we hand you the brand, a budget and the playbook, then get out of the way.

  1. 01numbers you ownusers, views, partnerships closed. that is what interviews are about.
  2. 02a networkcampus creators, org presidents, promoters, and the fomo team.
  3. 03budget and autonomyreal money for events, dinners and prizes. you decide how to spend it.
  4. 04a foot in the doortop performers get referred internally for internships and full-time roles.

03 · The five roles

read all five. apply to one.

a campus is not one person with a link. it is five, and each one owns a lever. you apply to one role, not a general pool — so read what each one actually is first.

  1. the lead role

    role 01

    campus president

    runs the team, and is the main line to fomo HQ.

    this suits you if

    • you are a frat exec, an org president, or whoever already runs things on campus
    • you know people across Greek life, teams and clubs
    • you can hold four people accountable without being annoying about it
    • you answer texts

  2. role 02

    growth

    gets people to download it and actually use it.

    this suits you if

    • you are the competitive one
    • walking up to a stranger or posting to 300 people does not faze you
    • you treat a number as a personal challenge
    • you have sold something before — tickets, merch, bids, anything

  3. role 03

    partnerships

    gets fomo into orgs, teams and events.

    this suits you if

    • you already know exec boards
    • you can text a social chair and get a reply
    • you keep a pipeline updated without being asked
    • you are comfortable on the phone and in meetings, not just DMs
  4. most applied for

    role 04

    content

    runs the school's page, mic in hand.

    this suits you if

    • you can film, edit, and hold a mic in front of a crowd without freezing
    • you already know what is going viral at your school
    • you post constantly on your own account
    • you are funny — that matters more here than being polished

  5. role 05

    culture

    makes the fomo dinners happen.

    this suits you if

    • you are the most socially plugged-in person on campus
    • you know every creator, DJ, promoter and personality by name — and they know you
    • you get invited to things
    • you could build a guest list from memory right now

One application, one role. Tell us which of the five you want and why you are the person for it — apply for a role.

04 · What fomo is

know what you're putting your name on.

a trading app for the rest of us.

you sign in with your Apple ID, buy and sell across Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana without bridging anything or paying gas, and you can see what the people you follow are actually holding.

that last part is the difference. every other trading app is a private spreadsheet — you have no idea whether the person telling you they are up 40% is up at all. on fomo the position is attached to the person.

it is not a game and it is not free money. real dollars go in and real dollars can be lost. what it is, for someone your age, is the first place your friends are all doing the same thing in public.

10 · How the team runs

six rules, all five of you.

none of this is about how hard you work. it is about the five of you not tripping over each other — a lead that nobody else hears about is a lead that dies.

  1. 01one group chateverything goes in it. no side deals, no silos.
  2. 02sunday team meetingtwenty minutes. the president runs it.
  3. 03sunday report to HQthe president sends it, using the template.
  4. 04ask before you promisenever commit fomo to money, prizes or sponsorships without approval. never guarantee an org anything HQ has not signed off on.
  5. 05post it, do not perfect itspeed beats polish every time.
  6. 06pass it across, same daycontent spots a big creator → culture. culture meets a frat president → partnerships. partnerships lands a 400-person event → growth and content.

The Sunday report. Every Sunday, drop your report in as a PDF or a spreadsheet. That is the whole thing.

12 · The part that is not up to you

most of this is your judgement. this part is not.

  1. say it when fomo paid

    the only rule about posting.

    nothing here is conditional on posting — not a dinner, not a prize. but when you post about something fomo paid for, say so in the post. “#ad”, “paid by fomo” or “fomo covered this” all work. a comment or the fourth line of a caption does not.

    • any post about something fomo paid for must say so, in the post itself.
    • undisclosed posts are rejected and count toward nothing.

  2. describe the app, never the outcome

    this is a trading app. the line matters.

    say what fomo is and say what you did. your own experience is yours to talk about. what you cannot do is suggest what anyone else will make.

    • never promise, imply or hint at returns — no “easy money”, no screenshot framed as what to expect.
    • a prize is a promotional competition prize. it is not a trading return.
    • never post someone else's positions without them saying yes.
    • never pitch anyone under 18, and never sign them up.

  3. use the name properly

    two things, and one catches everyone out.

    anything you post is yours and reads as yours. if something needs to come from fomo, ask and we will send it from fomo.

    • fomo is always lowercase. never Fomo, never FOMO — including at the start of a sentence.
    • do not make things that look official — no fake accounts, no profiles implying you speak for the company.

  4. what ends it

    short list, and none of it is about performance.

    a quiet term where nothing worked has never been a problem. every item here is about honesty, because the whole thing runs on people believing you.

    • undisclosed paid posts, after being told once.
    • presenting trading as a way to make guaranteed money.
    • faked numbers — bought engagement, invented signups, edited insights.
    • signing up anyone under 18, or anyone who did not ask.
